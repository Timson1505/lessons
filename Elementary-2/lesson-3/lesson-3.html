Intercultural Communication

I travel a lot, and during my journeys, I try to study people's behaviour, traditions and customs, history and so on. In this text, I wanted to breafly describe intercultural differences. I've visited many countries, and I’m fascinated by how differently people behave across cultures. What feels perfectly normal and friendly in one country can be surprisingly rude or even aggressive in another. Through my journeys, I’ve gradually learned to observe carefully, listen closely, and adapt respectfully.

One of the first things I noticed was eye contact. In countries like the United States or Germany, people usually look directly into your eyes when they speak. It feels confident and honest. However, when I visited Japan, I quickly realized that strong eye contact can be considered quite aggressive. People there often look slightly away to show respect, especially when speaking to someone older or more important.

Greetings are also incredibly diverse. In France or Italy, people warmly greet each other with kisses on the cheek, which feels very friendly and open. Meanwhile, in Japan, people politely bow instead of touching. The deeper the bow, the more respectful it is. I once tried to hug someone there, and it felt awkwardly inappropriate. In contrast, in more Western countries, a simple handshake is usually enough and considered perfectly polite.

Respect for elders is something I experienced very strongly in Central Asia and the Caucasus. In Kyrgyzstan, it is deeply respectful to give up your seat to older people or women in public transport. In Chechnya, people stand up when an elder enters the room, which is seen as a clear and honorable gesture. If you ignore these customs, it may be seen as surprisingly disrespectful.

Another interesting difference is smiling. In the United States, people smile frequently and openly, even at strangers. It creates a friendly and positive atmosphere. However, in Russia, smiling without a clear reason can seem slightly strange or even insincere. In Japan, people sometimes smile to hide discomfort, which can be a bit confusing at first.

Communication styles can also be very different. In Germany or the Netherlands, people tend to speak very directly. They clearly say what they think, and this is considered honest. But in countries like Japan or the United Kingdom, people often communicate more indirectly. They carefully choose softer words to avoid sounding rude. When I first experienced this, I sometimes misunderstood their meaning because it was expressed so subtly.

Traveling has taught me that cultural differences are not right or wrong—they are simply different. What matters is being open-minded, observant, and respectful. The more I travel, the more carefully I try to understand these differences, and the more deeply I appreciate the rich diversity of our world.

A Tractor VS Ferrari

It began in Italy, after world war 2. At that time, Italy needed rebuilding and vehicles for citizens - not tanks.

In a quiet but ambitious corner of Italy, a strong man named Ferruccio Lamborghini was building his future. He was not born in luxury, but he worked incredibly hard and became highly successful by producing tough and dependable trucks. His machines worked perfectly and were trusted everywhere. He also loved fast and powerful sports cars. He bought several Ferrari cars that were famous, elegant, and dangerously fast. But there was something wrong. They were unexpectedly fragile and worked badly at times. Finally, he decided to act confidently.

He went to meet Enzo Ferrari, a man who was proud, respected. Ferruccio spoke calmly but confidently. He suggested that Ferrari cars could be more stable, more comfortable. But Enzo Ferrari responded coldly and sharply.

He looked at Ferruccio with a hard and dismissive expression and said: “You are just a tractor maker. You do not understand real cars”. At that moment, everything changed.

Ferruccio felt suddenly angry. Ferruccio felt suddenly and intensely angry. His pride was deeply wounded, and his heart burned. That single anger did not break him—it transformed him. “I will build a car that is faster, stronger, and better than yours” - he replied. And he did.

Ferruccio worked day and night, pushing his team of brilliant and fearless engineers. The result was the first Lamborghini—a car that was wildly powerful, beautiful, and unbelievably fast. The world reacted immediately and loudly.

That was just the beginning of the legendary competition. Lamborghini and Ferrari competed aggressively, each trying to create cars that were more extreme and more advanced.

In the end, Ferruccio Lamborghini did not just build a car. He built a symbol of pride, and unstoppable ambition.

And he proved, powerfully and forever, that even a “simple” tractor maker could shake the world.
