What is the beauty?

Beauty is not a fixed idea—it changes across cultures, time periods, and social values. What one society considers attractive, another may not notice at all. In general, people often understand beauty as a combination of physical features (such as face, body, skin, and hair) and non-physical qualities like confidence, health, and personal style. Today, global media influences beauty standards, but local traditions and cultural identity still play a strong role.

In the 19th century, beauty in Europe was often associated with pale skin, delicate features, and a refined appearance. Pale skin showed that a person did not work outside, which was linked to higher social status. Both women and men tried to keep their skin light. In fact, some men used powders or light makeup to appear paler and more “noble.” A slim but not too thin body and neat clothing were also important.

In the 20th century, ideas of beauty began to change more quickly. In the early 1900s, pale skin was still popular, but later—especially after the 1920s—tanned skin became fashionable in some Western countries. This was partly influenced by figures like Coco Chanel, who helped make tanning a symbol of leisure and wealth. Over time, different body types, hairstyles, and fashion trends became popular in different decades, showing that beauty is always evolving.

In some cultures, beauty is connected to strength and health rather than delicacy. For example, among the Dinka people of South Sudan, tall height and strong bodies are often admired. Both men and women may decorate their bodies with patterns, scars, or jewelry, which are seen as signs of identity, courage, and beauty.

Different cultures have also had very unique beauty traditions. In China, especially in the past, small feet were considered very beautiful. This idea led to the practice of foot binding, where girls’ feet were tightly wrapped from a young age to keep them small. Although this tradition ended long ago, it shows how strongly cultural ideas can shape beauty standards.

In Myanmar (and also in parts of Thailand), some women from groups like the Kayan wear neck rings. The more rings they wear, the longer their neck appears, and this is traditionally seen as a sign of beauty and cultural identity.

Overall, beauty is not universal. It reflects history, environment, and values. By studying different cultures and time periods, we can see that beauty is not just about appearance—it is also about meaning, identity, and tradition.

The deaf musician

Ludwig van Beethoven is one of the most famous musicians in history, and his life is both inspiring and unusual. He was not blind, but he became deaf, which makes his achievements even more remarkable.

As a young man, Beethoven had dark, curly hair, which was often messier and wilder than his classmates’ more straight and tidy hair. While ome boys looked more well-dressed and cleaner, Beethoven’s appearance was often less neat but more expressive. His face was more serious than others, and he had fewer dimples but deeper lines (wrinkles) later in life. His skin was usually paler than many people around him, especially those who spent more time outdoors and were more tanned.

In terms of body, Beethoven was shorter and stockier than some of his classmates, who were sometimes taller and slimmer. He was not skinny, but also not very overweight—his build was stronger and more solid than average. As a young man, he looked less traditionally attractive than some others, but many people believed he was more interesting and expressive.

Beethoven learned music at a very early age. His father was stricter and more demanding than other fathers and pushed him to practice constantly. Because of this, Beethoven became more skilled and more disciplined than many of his classmates. While others were weaker or less focused, he was more serious and more hardworking.

As he grew older, Beethoven began to lose his hearing. Eventually, he became completely deaf, which made his life more difficult than that of other musicians. However, he did not stop composing. In fact, some of his later works are considered greater and more powerful than his earlier ones.

Even though he could not hear, Beethoven “heard” music in his mind. This made him more unique than other composers of his time. His life shows that a person can be stronger than their problems and greater than their limitations.

Overall, Beethoven may have been less fashionable, less neat, and less traditionally attractive than others, but he was more talented, more determined, and more unforgettable than most people in history.
