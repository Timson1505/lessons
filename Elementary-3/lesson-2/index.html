My experience in Dorm

When I moved to Europe to study at university, living in a dormitory (dorm) became a completely new experience for me. At first, I felt excited, but after a few weeks I experienced culture shock. I had to adapt to a new culture, different habits, and a completely different lifestyle. Living abroad also taught me persistence because adapting to a new environment is not always easy.

One of the biggest surprises was sharing a room with a roommate. In my dorm, two students live together in a small space, so we have to respect each other’s privacy and routines. My roommate prefers to study late at night, while I prefer studying in the morning. Sometimes this difference created problems because we both needed quiet time.

Another challenge was cleanliness. Some students are very neat, while others are extremely messy. In shared dormitories, students must organize cleaning schedules. Every week we have to clean the room, sweep the floor, and wash dishes. We also learned how to fold clothes properly because there is not enough storage space in the dorm. My roommate and I agreed that we mustn’t leave dirty clothes or food around the room.

Dormitory rules are also strict. Students must respect quiet hours, and the administration can forbid loud music or parties after 11 p.m. At first, I thought the rules were too strict, but later I understood that students should respect others who need to study or sleep.

Living in a dormitory has many advantages. First, students meet people from different countries and cultures. Second, dorm life helps students become independent because they have to cook, clean, and manage their own schedules. Students also improve communication skills because they constantly interact with roommates and classmates.

However, there are also some disadvantages. Privacy is limited, dorm rooms can become noisy, and shared kitchens are sometimes dirty. It can also be difficult when roommates have different personalities or lifestyles.

Despite the difficulties, I think living in a dormitory is an unforgettable experience. Students should try dorm life because it teaches responsibility, patience, independence, and cultural understanding. Living abroad helped me grow as a person and become more confident about the future.

Success: Talent or Hard Work?

Success is one of the most discussed topics in the world. Some people believe that talent is the key to achievement, while others argue that hard work is more important. But what really leads a person to success? Is it something people are born with, or is it something they build through effort and discipline?

Talented people often learn skills faster than others. A gifted musician, athlete, or scientist may show amazing abilities at a very young age. Because of this, many people think talent alone guarantees success. However, talent without effort can disappear. A person who has natural ability but does not practice regularly may never reach their full potential. Even talented students have to study, train, and improve themselves every day.

On the other hand, hard work can help ordinary people achieve extraordinary results. Many successful people were not considered talented at the beginning of their journey. They simply refused to give up. They understood that success does not happen overnight. Students who want to improve their knowledge should develop discipline and patience. They must learn from mistakes instead of being afraid of them.

At the same time, hard work alone may not always be enough. A person also needs opportunities, support, and confidence. Society should create conditions where everyone can discover their abilities. Teachers must encourage students to try different activities so they can find what they are good at.

Perhaps the real question is not “talent or hard work?” but “how can they work together?” Talent can open the door, but hard work helps people walk through it. Without effort, talent may remain hidden. Without talent, progress may be slower, but determination can still lead to success.
