The city that never sleeps

Fred's day starts early. He lives in a small but cozy apartment. Every morning, his alarm screams at 6:30 a.m. The first thing he does is to wash his face with cold water. This helps him wake up quickly. He doesn't smoke anymore—he quit two years ago because it was too expensive and bad for his health. Now, he prefers a quick breakfast. He knows that to achieve anything in this city, you need energy.

At 7:15 a.m., he leaves his building. The streets are already alive. There are always people rushing everywhere. Nobody drives in Manhattan if they want to get on time. Instead, he takes the subway. The train is crowded, but he manages to find a small space to stand. He puts on his headphones and talks to no one.

After work, Fred knows he needs balance. He goes to the gym three times a week. It is important for him. Sitting at a desk all day is not healthy, and he needs to manage his stress. Before he sleeps, Fred looks out his window. The city is still bright. There are taxis honking. There are people laughing. There are lights in many windows. New York never sleeps.

Fred thinks about his journey. He came from a small town in Ohio with little more than a portfolio and a big appetite for success. He had to overcome loneliness and self-doubt. But he never gave up. He learned to drive his own career forward. He learned to manage his time and his money. He learned to avoid the negativity that can sometimes fill this busy city.
